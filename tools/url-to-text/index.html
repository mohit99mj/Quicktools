URL to Text

Paste the link to a news article or blog post and get back clean, readable text — menus, ads, sidebars and popups stripped away. Perfect for saving articles or reading without clutter.

The page is fetched through a public proxy and processed in your browser. Nothing is stored on QuickTools servers.

How to use

  1. Paste the full URL of a news article or blog post (it must start with http:// or https://).
  2. Click Extract text and wait a few seconds while the page is fetched and cleaned.
  3. Read the clean article text, then copy it or download it as a .txt file.

Frequently asked questions

How does URL to Text work?

The tool fetches the page through a public proxy (browsers block direct cross-site fetching), removes scripts, menus, ads and sidebars, then picks the largest article-like text block — similar to a reader mode — and shows it as clean paragraphs.

Which pages work best?

News articles, blog posts and other long-form pages with a clear article body. Homepages, app dashboards and video pages usually don't have extractable article text.

Why did extraction fail for my link?

Some sites block automated fetching, require a login, sit behind a paywall, or render content with JavaScript that a plain fetch can't see. Try another link, or open the page in your browser and copy the text manually.

Is the page content sent anywhere?

The page is downloaded through a public CORS proxy so your browser can read it, then cleaned locally on the page. QuickTools doesn't save the URL or the extracted text.

Can it extract text from PDFs, images or videos?

No — this tool only reads web article pages (HTML). For PDFs try the PDF tools on QuickTools instead.

Does it bypass paywalls?

No. It only reads what the site serves to an anonymous visitor, the same as opening the link yourself. Paywalled or login-only content can't be extracted.

The result still has some menu text or ads — why?

Extraction is heuristic: every site structures its HTML differently. If a site uses unusual markup, a stray heading or link can slip through — just copy the paragraphs you need.
