Voice to Text

Turn your speech into written text instantly. Tap the mic, speak naturally, and watch your words appear live — in Hindi or English. Copy the transcript or download it as a text file.

QuickTools never stores your audio. Speech is converted to text by your browser's built-in speech service (Google's in Chrome); nothing is saved on our servers.

How to use

  1. Pick your language: Hindi, English (India) or English (US).
  2. Tap the mic button and allow microphone access when your browser asks.
  3. Speak naturally — confirmed words appear in black, words still being processed appear in grey.
  4. Tap the mic again to stop, then copy or download your transcript.

Frequently asked questions

Which browsers support voice typing?

Voice typing needs the Web Speech API, which works in Chrome and Edge on desktop and Android. Firefox, Safari and iPhones don't support it yet, so the tool shows a notice there instead of a broken mic button.

Is my voice recording uploaded or stored?

QuickTools doesn't record or store your audio. Your browser converts speech to text using its built-in speech service (Google's in Chrome), which needs an internet connection. Nothing is saved on QuickTools servers.

Can I dictate in Hindi?

Yes. Choose "Hindi (हिन्दी)" from the language menu before you start, then speak naturally in Hindi — the transcript appears in Devanagari script.

Why did it stop listening on its own?

Browsers pause recognition after a few seconds of silence. This tool restarts listening automatically while the mic is on; if it stops anyway, tap the mic to stop and start a fresh session.

It says the microphone is blocked — what do I do?

Click the lock (or tune) icon in your browser's address bar, open Site settings, set Microphone to "Allow", then reload the page and tap the mic again.

How do I add punctuation like commas?

Say the punctuation word out loud — for example say "comma", "full stop" or "question mark" in English — and the recognizer inserts it for you.

Does voice typing work offline?

No. The browser's speech service processes audio on remote servers, so you need an active internet connection while dictating.
