QR Code Generator

A QR code generator that turns any text or URL into a scannable QR code in seconds. Adjust the size, foreground and background colors, and the error-correction level, then download your code as a crisp PNG or an infinitely scalable SVG.

QR codes work for links, Wi-Fi details, contact cards and plain text — anything you can type can be encoded. Codes are generated locally with a widely used open-source QR library.

Your files never leave your device — everything runs in your browser.

How to use

  1. Type or paste the text or URL you want to encode.
  2. Pick an image size, foreground and background colors, and an error-correction level.
  3. Click "Generate QR code" to preview it instantly.
  4. Download the result as a PNG (for screens and documents) or SVG (for print).

Frequently asked questions

Are the QR codes free to use commercially?

Yes. The QR codes are generated entirely in your browser with no watermarks or tracking, so you can use them anywhere, including commercial projects.

What is error correction in a QR code?

Error correction lets a QR code stay readable even if part of it is damaged or covered. Higher levels (Q, H) survive more damage but store slightly less data. Level M works well for most uses.

Which size should I choose?

256 px is fine for screens; choose 512 or 1024 px for print so the code stays sharp when scaled up. The SVG download scales perfectly at any size.

Does this tool track what I encode?

No. Everything happens locally in your browser — your text or URL is never sent to a server.

How much text can a QR code hold?

A QR code can hold up to about 4,296 alphanumeric characters, but longer content makes a denser, harder-to-scan code. For URLs, keep them short — long links with tracking parameters scan less reliably, especially when printed small.

Why won't my QR code scan?

The most common causes are: printed too small (keep it at least 2 cm / 0.8 in wide), poor contrast (always dark code on a light background), or a damaged/creased print. Regenerate at 512 px or higher, leave a quiet margin around the code, and test it with your phone camera before printing in bulk.

Should I download PNG or SVG?

Choose PNG for screens, documents and social media — it works everywhere. Choose SVG for print, logos and signage: it's vector, so it stays razor-sharp at any size, from a business card to a billboard.
